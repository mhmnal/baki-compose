You are reviewing the visual design of a personal finance and shift-tracking app called Baki.

Do not review the code, business model or feature set. Review only what is visible in the screenshots.

## Product

- One user: a Grab driver in Malaysia.
- Baki tracks shift income, payouts, spending, bills, budgets, reserves and vehicle costs.
- The central question is: “What did this work actually pay, and what is still unresolved?”
- Phone is the primary surface. Desktop is a read-only companion.
- The app handles real money and messy reconciliation. It must feel calm, factual and trustworthy.

## Visual direction

- Material 3 Expressive.
- Warm “Roast & Pine” palette: deep green, cream, peach, muted surfaces.
- Archivo variable typography.
- Large rounded shapes, soft cards, restrained accents.
- Money is the hero when appropriate.
- No judgement, no shaming, no “good/bad” grades, no gamification.
- Dense financial information should still be scannable.

## What I want from the review

For each image:

1. What is the strongest visual decision?
2. What is the weakest visual decision?
3. Give exactly three concrete changes.
4. Rate the severity of each issue: low / medium / high.
5. Refer to specific elements, not generic advice.

Then review across all screens:

- hierarchy: can I find the most important number in under two seconds?
- information density: where is the screen too busy or too empty?
- type: size, weight, line length, numeric alignment
- color: is green/red used consistently and without judgement?
- cards and sections: too many nested surfaces? inconsistent radii?
- spacing: grouping, rhythm, alignment
- accessibility: contrast, text size, tap targets, small labels
- light/dark parity
- phone/desktop parity
- trust: does any screen look more certain than its data actually is?

Finally:

- Which screen is visually strongest?
- Which screen is visually weakest?
- What are the top five changes, in priority order?
- What should I deliberately keep unchanged?
- Which issue is a real usability problem rather than a matter of taste?

Do not propose a new visual identity. Critique this one.
Do not suggest new features.
Do not give a score without explaining it.
